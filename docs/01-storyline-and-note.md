# PART A — THE STORYLINE

The single sentence the whole thing hangs on:

> **India is not failing at stray management because it is spending too little. It is failing because it spends without a boundary, without a threshold, and without proof.**

## Beat 1 — Nobody knows the number

There has been no reliable national count of free-roaming dogs. The last official exercise was the 2019 Livestock Census, widely treated as an undercount; independent estimates now put the free-roaming dog population near **6.2 crore**. The Ministry itself has told Parliament it does not hold state-wise sterilisation totals, and there is **no national dashboard** tracking sterilisation or vaccination coverage in real time.

So every plan built on top of this is a guess. You cannot manage a population you have not counted, and you cannot claim progress against a denominator you do not have.

## Beat 2 — The 70% line is biology, not opinion

Population stabilisation requires sustained sterilisation coverage of roughly **70% of the dogs inside a defined area**. The Centre's own advisory to states says the same thing: cover at least 70%.

Below that line, the survivors breed back faster than the programme cuts. **69% is not "almost there" — it is close to zero.** A programme that sterilises 40% of a city produces a slightly smaller treadmill, not a smaller population. This is the most important idea in the entire pitch, and almost nobody outside the field states it plainly.

## Beat 3 — Money spread thin buys nothing

Do the arithmetic out loud. Roughly 6.2 crore dogs; 70% coverage means **on the order of 4.3 crore surgeries**. At the Centre's revised support of **₹800 per dog**, that is **thousands of crores** of work — and real delivered cost per surgery, once you include catching, transport, three-day post-op care and release, runs higher.

Against that, historical central disbursement under the AWBI birth-control scheme was often **under ₹50 lakh a year** before 2023. Even generous municipal budgets are small against the arithmetic: BMC has allocated about **₹23 crore** to sterilise **45,000 dogs a year** against a city population counted at **90,757**.

The conclusion is not "give us more money." It is sharper and it is the reason Love Paws should exist:

> **Any finite budget, spread evenly across a city, closes zero areas. The same budget, concentrated inside one boundary until it crosses 70%, closes that area permanently.** Coverage is not a percentage of money spent. It is a percentage of a defined map.

## Beat 4 — The dog that came back

Today the unit of payment is *the dog caught*. That is the wrong unit, and it creates every failure downstream:

- The same dog is caught, billed, and released twice, because there is no identity record.
- A dog is released in the wrong locality — which the ABC Rules 2023 explicitly prohibit — and the original area never actually loses a breeder.
- Post-operative recovery is compressed or skipped because the surgery is what gets paid for, and infection follows.
- Audits in Bhopal have surfaced **inflated figures, ghost surgeries and substandard facilities**.

None of this needs new law. It needs an **identity, a location and a timestamp attached to every animal**, and payment released against a verified release, not a claimed catch.

## Beat 5 — It has already been done, in India

**Goa.** Declared India's first rabies-controlled state in 2021 after three consecutive years with zero human rabies deaths. The method was not more money. It was:

- a **map divided into polygons**, each assigned to a team,
- a **smartphone app** recording every vaccination with GPS, offline-capable,
- a rule that a polygon is not finished until coverage inside it clears the threshold,
- education and surveillance running alongside — a One Health approach.

Over 95,000 dogs vaccinated at ~70% coverage, published in *Nature Communications*. **The demonstration already exists. What does not exist is the repeatable format that lets any municipal body run it.** That is the gap Love Paws fills.

## Beat 6 — The Love Paws bet

Not another shelter. Not another rescue account.

> **Take one defined area. Map it. Fund it against its own statistics. Run catch–sterilise–recover–release with every animal traceable. Close it out past 70%. Publish the evidence. Then hand the format — SOPs, app, cost model, staffing norms — to the state as something that can be copied.**

One ward proved is worth more to Indian policy than ten thousand untraceable surgeries. That is the BHAG: not to sterilise the most dogs, but to **produce the operating system that everyone else uses.**

**Mumbai (the natural pilot city):** 90,757 stray dogs counted in 2024 · over **1.28 lakh** bites in 2024 · **8 ABC centres** for the whole city · BMC target 45,000 sterilisations a year and 1,35,000 over three years, ~₹23 crore allocated · 4,34,529 cumulative sterilisations recorded up to August 2025.

The gap between **54 reported** rabies deaths and **~5,726 estimated** is the surveillance story in one line. We are not measuring the thing we claim to be solving.

## 2. Where the law and the courts now stand

**ABC Rules, 2023** mandate:
- Catch–Neuter–Vaccinate–Return (CNVR); surgical sterilisation with females prioritised
- Return to the **exact original location**; relocation of healthy dogs is prohibited
- **Ear-notching** or marking of every sterilised animal
- Multi-tier **monitoring committees**
- SOPs for capture, surgery documentation and population survey
- Only **AWBI-recognised** organisations may perform ABC work (certificate valid 3 years)

**Central funding, revised 2025:** ₹800 per dog (up from ₹445), ₹600 per cat, plus a one-time ₹2 crore infrastructure grant for state veterinary hospitals. Advisory to states: cover **at least 70%** of strays.

**Supreme Court, 2025:**
- **11 Aug 2025** — ordered stray dogs removed from Delhi-NCR streets to shelters within eight weeks, with no release back. Widely criticised as unimplementable and inhumane.
- **22 Aug 2025** — order modified: dogs picked up must be **released back into their own territory after sterilisation and immunisation**, except those rabid or displaying aggression.
- All states and UTs impleaded, with a pan-India stray dog policy under consideration.

**Why this matters to us:** a national policy is being written *right now*. The window for a demonstrated, evidence-backed operating model to influence it is open and will not stay open. This is the single strongest argument for moving in the next two quarters rather than next year.

## 3. What the government programme actually is

Responsibility sits with **urban local bodies and state governments**, funded largely from municipal budgets, with the Centre providing per-animal support and advisories. Delivery is almost entirely **outsourced to NGOs and private contractors**, paid per animal, with weak or absent municipal supervision. AWBI recognises the organisations; nobody continuously verifies the output.

## 4. Failures — expanded against Vishal's four points

### 4.1 Budget: poor allocation and management of resources

*(On the ₹200 crore figure: a national ₹200 crore ABC allocation could not be confirmed from public record — see §10. The argument below does not depend on the exact figure, and is stronger without it.)*

**(a) Leakage of funds.** Payment is made per animal claimed, against paper records, with no independent verification of identity or release. Audits in Bhopal found **inflated numbers, ghost surgeries and substandard facilities**. Because no animal carries a durable identity record, a claim is effectively unfalsifiable — and an unfalsifiable claim will always be inflated.

**(b) Poorly qualified and trained personnel.** There is a documented shortage of trained veterinary staff and technical capacity across most districts. Catching is the least regulated and highest-injury part of the chain, and is usually done by the least trained people on the payroll. The consequences show up as post-operative infection, mortality, and public hostility to the programme.

**(c) No SOPs in place.** Few fully functional district-level ABC centres exist, and project recognition of existing centres is incomplete. Where SOPs exist on paper, nothing forces adherence, because nothing measures it.

**(d) The allocation problem underneath all three.** Even if every rupee were spent honestly, spreading it uniformly across a city guarantees sub-70% coverage everywhere and stabilisation nowhere. **Poor allocation is not only theft; it is also honest money spent without a boundary.**

### 4.2 Area management

No systematic, area-wise ownership of the assignment. No ward is anybody's responsibility to *finish*. Work is measured in animals processed, never in **territory closed out**, so no area ever reaches the threshold at which the population actually stops replacing itself.

### 4.3 Random catch and release

Dogs released in the wrong locality (contrary to the ABC Rules), re-caught animals billed twice, no traceability from catch to release, no way to prove an individual animal was ever sterilised, vaccinated or returned home. Without traceability there is no coverage figure — only an invoice.

### 4.4 Centres and post-operative care

Municipal bodies cannot find appropriately equipped centres. Mumbai runs **8 centres** for a city with roughly **90,000+** dogs. Under-equipped theatres and compressed recovery periods produce post-operative infection — which is both an animal-welfare failure and the fastest way to lose community consent for the entire programme.

## 5. Solutions — the model

**1. Map and define areas with statistics.**
Divide the target territory into **micro-zones** (polygon per lane cluster / society cluster, not per ward). Each zone gets a baseline: estimated dog count, feeder/caregiver contacts, bite-incident history, conflict hotspots. This is done once, by survey, and then it is the denominator for everything.

**2. Budget and deploy against zone statistics.**
Fund the zone, not the calendar. Money is committed to a zone with an explicit target: cross 70% sterilisation coverage inside this boundary, then move. Sequencing is by bite-incidence and density, not by convenience.

**3. Technology as the control layer, not a website.**
Every animal gets a record at the moment of catch: geotag, photograph, ear-notch/marking ID, sex, condition. The record follows it through surgery, recovery and release. The app is a **chain of custody**, and its by-product is the coverage number nobody currently has.

**4. Track activity and release — and pay on verified outcome.**
Payment milestone shifts from *animal caught* to **animal released, at the recorded catch location, with photographic and GPS proof, after the mandated recovery period.** This one change removes most of the leakage without needing any new regulation.

**5. Build the centres, the ecosystem and the people.**
Right-sized surgical capacity with real post-op recovery space; trained and certified catchers, not casual labour; veterinary protocol and infection audit; community caregivers integrated as the field network rather than treated as obstructions.

**6. Vaccination in the same motion.**
Anti-rabies vaccination rides on the same visit and the same record. Sterilisation is a multi-year population play; **vaccination coverage cuts human deaths within a single season.** Pharmaceutical partners can be brought in against a government directive to supply at scale, which is a far easier ask when the coverage data is auditable.

## 6. Love Paws Foundation — goals

1. Reduce the stray animal population through **humane sterilisation**.
2. Reduce the incidence of **human–stray conflict**.
3. Increase **anti-rabies vaccination** drives, with pharmaceutical company commitments secured through government directive.
4. Establish **practices, methods, formats and protocols** for sterilisation coverage in smaller locations and villages — where there is currently no model at all.
5. **Reduce pressure on community caregivers**, who are today absorbing the cost, the labour and the hostility of a failed public programme.

## 7. BHAG — the action plan

**Establish a demonstrative platform** that proves the way forward is doable, and use it to reduce the all-India incidence of strays and human–stray conflict.

Concretely, that means:

- **Prove it in one area.** One ward or one cluster, taken from baseline to >70% coverage, fully traceable, with bite-incidence measured before and after. The deliverable is not the dogs; it is **the evidence pack**.
- **Package the format.** SOPs, app, cost-per-outcome model, staffing norms, centre specification, audit method — assembled as something a municipal commissioner can adopt without redesigning anything.
- **Take it to policy.** Enter the pan-India policy conversation now open at the Supreme Court and in the states with a working model rather than a position paper.

1. **The ₹200 crore figure.** A national ₹200 crore ABC allocation is not confirmable from public sources. Is this a state figure, a BMC/Maharashtra figure, or from a document we hold? It should either be sourced or replaced before it appears in anything public — a contestable number in the first slide costs us the room.
2. **Pilot geography.** Mumbai ward, or a smaller municipal body where a full close-out is achievable within one budget cycle and the win is unambiguous? A tier-2 city or a taluka may prove the *format* faster and cheaper than a Mumbai ward, and Goal 4 explicitly points at smaller locations.
3. **Positioning.** Does Love Paws operate as an implementer, or as the policy-and-technology layer that municipalities and existing NGOs run on? The two need different teams, different funding and different websites. The current draft leans deliberately towards the second.

## 12. App strategy — one-page outline (for the next step)

**The app is not an app for donors. It is the chain of custody.**

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| **Zone map** | Polygons with baseline counts, caregiver contacts, incident history, live coverage % |
| **Field capture** | Catch record: geotag, photo, sex, condition, ear-notch/marking ID. Works offline; syncs later |
| **Clinical record** | Surgery, vet ID, vaccination batch, complications |
| **Recovery** | Mandated post-op window enforced by the workflow, not by trust |
| **Release** | Geotagged, photographed release within a permitted radius of the catch point — the ABC Rules requirement made mechanical |
| **Verification & payment** | Milestone released only on a complete chain. Removes the leakage at its source |
| **Public dashboard** | Coverage by zone, bites by zone, vaccination by zone. The national number that does not currently exist |
| **Caregiver channel** | Report, request, track — turning the community into the sensing network |

Design principles to hold: **offline-first** (field areas have no reliable data), **one-hand operable** (the operator is holding a dog), **photo-and-GPS as the only accepted evidence**, and **the polygon, not the animal, is the unit of completion.**
