Field note 01 / Stray management in India

We are not short of money. We are short of a boundary.

India runs sterilisation programmes without a count, without a threshold and without proof. Love Paws Foundation proposes the opposite: a defined area, a measured population, and every animal traceable from catch to release.

Scroll

The storyline

The argument, in six beats.

India is not failing at stray management because it is spending too little. It is failing because it spends without a boundary, without a threshold, and without proof.

  1. Beat 1 Nobody knows the number No reliable national count, no state-wise totals, no dashboard. Every plan built on top of this is a guess.
  2. Beat 2 The 70 per cent line is biology, not opinion Below the threshold, survivors breed back faster than the programme cuts. Sixty-nine per cent is close to zero.
  3. Beat 3 Money spread thin buys nothing Any budget spread evenly across a city closes zero areas. The same budget inside one boundary closes it permanently.
  4. Beat 4 The dog that came back Paying per animal caught produces double billing, wrong-locality releases and skipped recovery. The wrong unit of payment creates every failure downstream.
  5. Beat 5 It has already been done, in India Goa, Jaipur, Sikkim and Ahmedabad each got there with a map, a phone and one rule: finish the polygon. The demonstrations exist. The repeatable format does not.
  6. Beat 6 The Love Paws bet Take one area, close it out past the threshold with every animal traceable, publish the evidence, and hand the format to policy.

01 / The scale

01b / The count

The official number says the problem is shrinking.

India's Livestock Census counted 1.71 crore stray dogs in 2012 and 1.53 crore in 2019, a decline of roughly 11 per cent. Over the same period, reported dog bites rose relentlessly. Independent ecological estimates put the free-roaming population near 6.2 crore, four times the official figure.

Both cannot be true. Until one of them is settled, no coverage percentage anyone quotes has a denominator, and a programme cannot report progress against a number it does not have.

Where the burden actually sits

Five states carried roughly half of India's reported dog bites in 2024. Maharashtra alone recorded more bites than most countries record in a decade. This is the argument for sequencing: the map is not uniform, so the money should not be either.

02 / The threshold

Sixty-nine per cent is not almost.

Population stabilisation requires sustained sterilisation coverage of about 70 per cent of the dogs inside a defined area. The Centre's own advisory to states says the same. 03 / The arithmetic

Spread thin, any budget buys nothing.

Roughly 6.2 crore dogs. Seventy per cent coverage means on the order of 4.3 crore surgeries. At the Centre's revised support of ₹800 per dog, that is thousands of crores of work, and real delivered cost per surgery runs higher once catching, transport, three days of post-operative care and release are counted.

Against that arithmetic, central disbursement under the AWBI birth control scheme was often under ₹50 lakh a year before 2023. Municipal budgets are small too: BMC has allocated about ₹23 crore to sterilise 45,000 dogs a year.

The conclusion is not "give us more money".

Any finite budget, spread evenly across a city, closes zero areas. The same budget, concentrated inside one boundary until it crosses 70 per cent, closes that area permanently. Coverage is not a percentage of money spent. It is a percentage of a defined map.

No plausible budget does every ward at once, which is precisely why the money has to finish one boundary before it moves to the next.

04 / Where it breaks

Four failures, one root.

01

Money without a boundary, and money without a check

Payment is made per animal claimed, against paper records, with no independent verification of identity or release. Audits in Bhopal have surfaced inflated figures, ghost surgeries and substandard facilities. Because no animal carries a durable identity record, a claim is effectively unfalsifiable, and an unfalsifiable claim will always be inflated. Underneath the leakage sits a quieter problem: even honest money, spread uniformly, guarantees sub-threshold coverage everywhere and stabilisation nowhere.

Poor allocation is not only theft. It is also honest money spent without a boundary.

02

No area is anybody's job to finish

There is no systematic, area-wise ownership of the assignment. Work is measured in animals processed, never in territory closed out, so no area ever reaches the point at which the population stops replacing itself. A city can run a programme for twenty years, process lakhs of animals, and still not be able to name one locality that is finished.

Documented gap: no national management information system or real-time dashboard publicly tracks sterilisation or vaccination coverage.

03

Random catch and release

Dogs are released in the wrong locality, which the ABC Rules 2023 expressly prohibit. Re-caught animals are billed twice. There is no traceability from catch to release and no way to prove an individual animal was ever sterilised, vaccinated or returned home. Without traceability there is no coverage figure, only an invoice.

The unit of payment is the dog caught. That single choice creates every failure downstream.

04

Centres that cannot carry the load

Municipal bodies cannot find appropriately equipped centres. Mumbai runs eight for a city counted at over ninety thousand dogs. Few fully functional district level ABC centres exist and recognition of the existing ones is incomplete. Under-equipped theatres and compressed recovery windows produce post-operative infection, which is both an animal welfare failure and the fastest way to lose community consent for the whole programme.

Add to this a documented shortage of trained veterinary personnel, and catching work handed to the least trained people on the payroll.

05 / The model

Fund the map, not the calendar.

01

Map and define areas with statistics

Divide the territory into micro-zones, a polygon per lane cluster rather than per ward. Each zone carries a baseline: estimated dog count, caregiver contacts, bite incident history, conflict hotspots. Surveyed once, then used as the denominator for everything that follows.

02

Budget against zone statistics

Money is committed to a zone with an explicit target: cross 70 per cent inside this boundary, then move. Sequencing follows bite incidence and density, not convenience.

03

Technology as the control layer

Every animal gets a record at the moment of catch. The record follows it through surgery, recovery and release. Its by-product is the coverage number that does not currently exist anywhere.

04

Track activity and release, and pay on verified outcome

The payment milestone moves from animal caught to animal released, at the recorded catch location, with photographic and GPS proof, after the mandated recovery period. This one change removes most of the leakage without needing a single new regulation.

05

Build the centres, the ecosystem, the people

Right-sized surgical capacity with real post-operative recovery space. Trained and certified catchers rather than casual labour. Veterinary protocol and infection audit. Community caregivers integrated as the field network instead of treated as obstructions.

06

Vaccination in the same motion

Anti-rabies vaccination rides on the same visit and the same record. Sterilisation is a multi-year population play. Vaccination coverage cuts human deaths within a single season, and auditable data makes the ask to pharmaceutical partners a far easier one.

06 / The platform

Not an app for donors. A chain of custody.

Each stage hands the next one a record it cannot fake. Break any link and the payment does not release.

01

Catch

Geotag, photograph, sex, condition, marking ID. Works offline, syncs later.

02

Surgery

Vet ID, procedure, vaccination batch, complications logged against the same record.

03

Recovery

Mandated post-operative window enforced by the workflow, not by trust.

04

Release

Geotagged and photographed inside a permitted radius of the catch point.

05

Verify

Complete chain or no claim. Duplicate animals surface automatically.

06

Publish

Coverage, bites and vaccination by zone, on a dashboard anyone can audit.

Design constraints held throughout: offline first, because field areas have no reliable data. One-hand operable, because the operator is holding a dog. Photograph and GPS as the only accepted evidence. And the polygon, not the animal, as the unit of completion.

One animal, one record, six timestamps

This is the whole system in one card. A claim is only payable when every field is filled by the person who did the work, at the moment they did it, with the phone's own clock and coordinates. A dog caught twice shows up as a duplicate. A dog released a kilometre away fails the radius check. A recovery window cut from three days to three hours fails on the timestamps.

None of this requires new law. The ABC Rules already mandate return to the exact original location, ear-notching and record keeping. It requires only that somebody check, and checking is cheap once the record exists.

06b / Why vaccination comes first

Two places to cut the same chain.

Dogs are the source of about 99 per cent of human rabies cases. Once symptoms appear the disease is effectively always fatal. Before that point it is entirely preventable, twice over.

Sterilisation is the long game. It shrinks the reservoir over years, and it is the only thing that ends the cycle rather than managing it. Vaccination is the fast one: push coverage past 70 per cent inside a zone and human deaths from that zone stop within a single season, whether or not a single dog has been sterilised yet.

Which is why they belong on the same visit and in the same record. Every catch is already a vaccination opportunity, and every vaccination round is already a census. Running them separately, as most programmes do, pays the catching cost twice and produces two sets of numbers that never reconcile.

07 / It has been done

Goa already proved the method.

Declared India's first rabies controlled state in 2021 after three consecutive years with zero human rabies deaths. The method was not more money. It was a map divided into polygons, each assigned to a team; a smartphone app recording every vaccination with GPS, working offline; a rule that a polygon is not finished until coverage inside it clears the threshold; and education and surveillance running alongside. Over 95,000 dogs vaccinated at roughly 70 per cent coverage, published in Nature Communications.

The demonstration exists. What does not exist is the repeatable format that lets any municipal body run it. That is the gap Love Paws fills.

07b / How Goa actually did it

Eight years, one method, repeated.

Not a breakthrough. A sequence, held to for long enough that the arithmetic caught up.

2013

Campaign begins with the state government

A small pilot vaccination drive. At this point no location-specific canine rabies surveillance data existed anywhere in Goa, so the first job was building the map, not vaccinating against it.

March 2014

A 24 hour rabies hotline

One public number, widely advertised, for reporting a suspect rabid animal at any hour. This is the piece most programmes skip. Without it there is no way to know whether the vaccination is working.

2014 to 2019

Polygon by polygon, on repeat

Teams were directed to assigned polygons shown on a smartphone app, each dog logged with GPS, working offline and syncing later. The entire state was re-vaccinated every twelve months, with a peak of more than 25,000 dogs in a single month.

2019

Canine rabies collapses

A 92 per cent reduction in monthly canine rabies cases against the start of the campaign. The reservoir, not just the symptoms.

June 2021

First Rabies Controlled Area in India

Declared after three consecutive years with zero human rabies deaths.

2022

Published, and taught

The results appear in Nature Communications, making the method citable in a policy room. By June 2022 the education programme had reached five million children with rabies prevention lessons.

Today

Over four million doses

Delivered in Goa since 2013, the largest continuous rabies control effort ever run in India.

Four mechanisms carried all of it: a boundary drawn before any work started, a phone that recorded every animal where it stood, a rule that a polygon is not finished until it clears the threshold, and a surveillance line that told them the truth while it was still fixable.

07c / Three more, already done

Goa is not a fluke.

Every one of these ran in India, on Indian budgets, under Indian conditions, solving catching, transport and post-operative care exactly as we will have to.

JaipurHelp in Suffering / since 1994

7.2 to 2.2Dog bites per 1,000 people

One of the longest continuously running ABC programmes in the world, built on WHO and WSPA guidelines. More than 1,50,000 dogs have passed through it. Over 70 per cent of female street dogs are sterilised, and over 70 per cent of the street dog population is vaccinated.

It is credited with averting an estimated 500 human rabies deaths and 3,60,000 bite injuries. Human rabies in the city is now close to zero. Thirty years is also the honest answer to how long a city takes when it starts without a map.

SikkimSARAH programme / since 2005

4 to 0Human rabies deaths per year

The first government-sanctioned programme in India to put animal welfare and public health inside one structure, which is One Health before the phrase was common. Four human rabies deaths in 2005, then none at all from 2006 through 2015.

Two deaths appeared in 2016, both near the West Bengal border. That detail matters more than the zeros. It is the case for drawing boundaries around whole administrative units rather than convenient pockets, because a partially covered neighbour re-seeds you.

AhmedabadMunicipal ABC programme

Near 90%Sterilisation coverage

1,88,828 sterilisations delivered against roughly ₹18.11 crore of spending, which works out near ₹960 an animal. A major Indian city approaching the threshold on a municipal budget.

It is also the cleanest evidence available that surgical cost is not what stops the rest of the country. Ahmedabad paid roughly what everybody else is offered. What it did differently was keep going in the same place.

07d / The policy clock

The law is not the missing piece.

Almost everything this page argues for is already policy. It has been written, funded, revised and given a deadline. What was never built is the machinery that shows whether any of it happened.

1960

Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act

The parent statute. Every rule since is made under it.

2001

First ABC (Dogs) Rules

Catch, neuter, vaccinate, return becomes national policy. Delivery is left to urban local bodies, and so is the measurement.

2021

NAPRE, and Zero by 30

India commits to eliminating dog-mediated human rabies by 2030, aligned to the global target set by WHO, FAO, WOAH and GARC. The interim goals were explicit: cut dog bites by half by 2025, and raise dog vaccination by 75 per cent by 2025.

2023

ABC Rules revised

Return to the exact original location, ear-notching, multi-tier monitoring committees, AWBI recognition valid three years. The traceability this page asks for is, on paper, already compulsory.

2025

Funding raised

₹800 per dog, up from ₹445, plus a one-time ₹2 crore infrastructure grant for state veterinary hospitals. States are advised to cover at least 70 per cent.

Aug 2025

The Supreme Court intervenes

Removal from Delhi NCR streets ordered on 11 August, modified on 22 August to release back into the same territory after sterilisation and immunisation, except animals that are rabid or aggressive. All states and union territories were impleaded for a pan-India policy.

Now

The 2025 targets came due

Bites were to fall by half. They rose from 37,17,336 in 2024 to 47,48,478 in 2025, an increase of roughly 28 per cent. No national dashboard exists to explain why, because none was ever built.

2030

The deadline

Four years out. Reaching it needs coverage that can be proved, zone by zone, which is the one thing nobody currently produces.

A 50% cut asked for. A 28% rise delivered.NAPRE dog bite target, 2025

This is the gap Love Paws exists to close, and it is not a gap in intent, funding or law. It is a gap in verification. A target with no measurement system is a press release, and the country has now run that experiment for four years with the deadline in sight.

09 / The big goal

Prove one area. Package the format. Take it to policy.

A pan-India stray dog policy is being written right now, after the Supreme Court's August 2025 orders brought every state and union territory into the matter. The window for a demonstrated, evidence-backed operating model is open, and it will not stay open.

Prove it in one area

One ward or cluster, taken from baseline to past 70 per cent, fully traceable, with bite incidence measured before and after. The deliverable is not the dogs. It is the evidence pack.

Package the format

SOPs, app, cost per outcome, staffing norms, centre specification, audit method. Assembled so a municipal commissioner can adopt it without redesigning anything.

Take it to policy

Enter the national conversation with a working model rather than a position paper. One ward proved is worth more than ten thousand untraceable surgeries.
